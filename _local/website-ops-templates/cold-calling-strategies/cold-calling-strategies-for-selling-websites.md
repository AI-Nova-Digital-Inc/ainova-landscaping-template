## Why Most People Fail at Cold Calling

- Talk too much
- Script every single word — sounds robotic
- Sound like every other salesperson calling the same business owner
- Get hung up on → lose motivation → quit
- Cold calling is hard, but it is the **fastest way to get clients**

---

## The Core Mindset

- Expect rejection — it is normal
- Getting nervous is natural; push through it
- The skill compounds: better at cold calls → better at actual sales presentations → more clients
- Volume matters — go down the list, mark who answered, who didn't, move on

---

## Lead with Value — The Website Hook

**The Strategy:**
- Tell them you already built them a website and you want to show it to them
- You haven't actually built anything yet — you're just qualifying interest
- If they say yes → book the meeting → import a template → put their logo on it (takes under 5 minutes)
- Present it as a "first draft"

**If they hate the template:**
> "No problem — out of all these samples, which one do you like most? I'll get it recreated."

- Gets your foot in the door regardless
- You're now in a conversation with a warm prospect

## Build and Use a Lead List

- Use a spreadsheet — not Google Maps listing one by one
- Mark: answered / didn't answer / callback needed
- Lead generation tools provide:
  - Phone numbers
  - Social media profiles
  - Google / Yelp review counts and ratings
  - Whether they have a website or not
  - Whether Google/Facebook pixels are installed

**Use this data during the call:**
- Mention their review count
- Mention their rating
- Mention if their listing shows "closed" or no website
- Makes the call feel custom, not copy-paste

## Tonality — Sound Human, Not Robotic

### What NOT to do

**Too stiff and salesy:**
> "Hey, is this Bob? Yes. Hey Bob, this is Pavle calling from ABC Web Design Services and I am just wondering if you would be interested in some kind of website." *[click]*

**Too formal and scripted:**
> "Hey, good afternoon, how are you doing today? Awesome, that's great to hear. So I was just calling because I noticed your website was just a little bit outdated and your mobile indexing was set up incorrectly." *[click]*

### What TO do

- Sound like a genuine person, not a salesperson
- Ask questions rather than pitch statements
- Match the energy of the industry you're calling into
  - Home services (plumbers, landscapers) → casual, even a little rough around the edges
  - Professional services → slightly more polished, but still conversational

**Casual home services example:**
> "Yo, is this Bob?" → sounds like a customer or friend → gets a real response

> "Yo Bob, what's going on man. Dude I was just looking at your website — it's insane, I tried to find some of your past projects and you don't even look like a real business Bob, what's going on man?"

- Sounds human
- Creates curiosity
- Not threatening or salesy

## What to Say — Script Examples

### When they have no website or weak online presence

> "Yo Bob, so I was just looking up some landscaping services, dude I tried to look at your past projects — do you have anything I can look at?"

*(Let them explain — maybe they say "I have an Instagram")*

> "Okay, sounds good Bob. Look dude, I'll be honest — I saw that and I know how powerful it is to actually show that online. I actually built you a website. Do you want me to just show it to you? I went ahead man — I like your business, you've got some freaking good reviews, I figured you'd like this. Do you want to see it or not?"

## Handling Prospects Who Already Have a Website

- Always possible to find something broken or improvable within seconds
- Common things to mention:
  - Slow image loading speed
  - Phone number not at the top of the page
  - Contact form misaligned or broken
  - Low quality gallery photos
  - Facebook or Google pixel not installed
  - No live chat / calendar booking on contact form

**Approach:**
> "Yo Bob, so I was just looking at your site man — I found a couple things broken. You guys are still open and still in business, right?"

*(They confirm)*

> "Well I saw that this pixel wasn't properly installed and it wasn't really showing up on my phone. So what I actually did Bob — I built you a brand new site. I don't know if you were looking for it, but I wanted to fix the issue for you, just give you some value up front. Would you be free sometime to look at it?"

**If they push back ("I already have a website"):**
> "You don't have to use it if you want — I can even show you how to fix it on your existing site. Does that sound good?"

- Make it **low risk** — no pressure, no commitment
- While dialing, load their website live — reference exact things you see on it to sound specific

## The Goal of the Cold Call

> **The goal is NOT to sell web design on the call.**

- Do NOT pitch packages, templates, or prices on the cold call
- The only goal: **book the demo/sales call**
- Sell the meeting, not the service

---

## The Demo Call — How to Make It Irresistible

Give the prospect a strong reason to show up. Promise real value:

> "Bob, I'm going to do a full audit of your website on this call. I'll look at everything you're doing, find the errors, and see if I can get some of them fixed for you. I'm also going to pull up all your competitors' websites and show you what they're doing — their Google reviews, whether they're advertising on Yelp, Google, or Facebook. I'll show you behind the scenes. Does that sound good?"

**Prepare 10 minutes before the meeting:**
- Look at their website
- Pull up 2–3 competitor websites
- Note review counts, gaps, missing features

**On the demo call, deliver real value:**
- "Your competitors have 70 reviews — you have 7. Here's how to automatically email clients a review link."
- "Your phone number is at the bottom of the page — move it to the top."
- "Your contact form doesn't link to a calendar — you're losing leads."
- "You have no live chat — that's another missed contact opportunity."

- Make them feel the free call alone was worth $50–$100
- Once they're engaged and trust is built → pitch your services, packages, and pricing

## Summary {#summary}

| Principle | Key Point |
|---|---|
| Lead with value | Offer a pre-built website draft to open the conversation |
| Use a lead list | Spreadsheet with data — don't cold browse Google Maps |
| Personalize each call | Mention reviews, ratings, specific site issues |
| Sound human | Casual tone, questions over statements, match industry energy |
| Handle existing websites | Find something broken — there always is something |
| Goal of the call | Book the demo call, not close the sale |
| Make demo irresistible | Promise audit, competitor research, actionable advice |
| Overdeliver on demo | Give real value first — pricing comes after trust is built |
